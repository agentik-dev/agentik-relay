# agent-relay design: orchestrator rows (r1)

**Status:** design r1, 2026-09-29, by a helper in sitting example-mac 2026-09-28-6. It is revised once after its refuter round: each blocking finding was checked against the code at `9d20a0e`, the two briefs and the ledgers before it was fixed. ar32a is built (2026-09-29), and so is ar32b, less the two parts §11 names; ar32c is not. Inputs: the owner's ask of 2026-09-29, relay-r1 §1 and §3 to §9, the example-app and example-ops briefs of 2026-09-29, and the ledger entries it names. The portfolio's relay entries now live in example-ops's `DECISIONS.md`, so this record names them by title.

## 1. The problem and the design

Today the example-app and example-ops orchestrators run as attended sessions under `/loop`. At the 80% checkpoint they write `RESPAWN` and stop, and the owner restarts them by hand. The relay already spawns fresh headless sittings, gates them and merges them. Four gaps keep the orchestrators off it: RULES forbid helpers, a repeated row stops the night `no-progress`, a child cannot start a night, and a headless sitting cannot show the selector.

The design adds one row kind, `kind: orchestrator`. The relay restarts it fresh after each green, merged sitting, and its no-progress key is the charter's Next act. Between its sittings the relay runs the build rows it added, one child at a time. The owner starts the night; the orchestrator adds rows, and the relay re-reads the queue from main.

**Scope.** One night serves one repo (fork 1). example-app moves onto the relay first. example-ops stays attended while its program is the cross-repo moves (fork 4). example-ops also has no queue yet, and its check, `scripts/verify-all`, reads live services that the pinned env cannot reach. Releases, tags and device work stay with the owner or an attended sitting: the row's `device` is `never`, and the relay pushes only `main`.

## 2. The row kind and its queue fields

```yaml
  - id: orient
    kind: orchestrator            # build (the default) or orchestrator
    prompt: history/sessions/PROMPT-2026-09-29-orient.md   # the standing brief
    sections: [How this runs, How decisions are made here, "Settled; record, do not ask"]
    model: claude-fable-5-1       # or Opus 5.5 on [1m]
    verify: make check-fast       # the merge's verify line, per row for this kind
    may_touch: [Makefile, scripts/]   # guarded paths this row may also change
    push: never                   # or main (section 7)
    push_check: make check
    budget: {turns: 400, context: 400000, usd: 150, wall: 6h}
```

`queue.Validate` allows at most one orchestrator row per queue. That row has no `packet`, `depends_on` or `base`, and no row depends on it. Its merge policy is always `merge-on-green`, its `device` is always `never`, and its packet copy-in is declared skipped (agent-relay entry 1). `prompt` must exist at main. `sections` lists the brief's sections the body line names. A build row is today's row, unchanged.

## 3. How a sitting starts

Stdin has four parts:

1. **The Every session block**, read at main at the night's start (AR30) and again at each queue re-read.
2. **RULES for orchestrator rows**, a second embedded file. It keeps today's commit, push, device and soft-ceiling sentences and drops act 0. Its headless sentence reads: "You are headless (`claude -p`): the sitting ends when your turn ends. Never start a background command or a background agent, and never end a turn waiting for a notification." Its helper sentence reads: "You may use helpers, in-process subagents, in the foreground only: the turn that starts one waits for its result. A helper writes files in your worktree and never commits; only you commit." Further sentences cover the charter (`RELAY_ROW` already holds the row id, so never assign it), the queue edit, merging green build branches, questions, and the Next act: a concrete step, `DONE: <why>` or `WAITING: <what>`, never a wait on another repo. The precedence sentence reads: "These rules win over the whole brief, its How this runs and Driving the relay sections included: never use `/loop` or a timer, never start or stop a night or run a measuring plan, never merge into main or run git in the root checkout, and never push."
3. **The CONTEXT line**, its own format and so its own template hash. It names the row and sitting number, the worktree, branch, main's sha, the brief and the charter directory. It names the newest charter at main with its Next act, and the ceilings. It names the green build branches waiting for this sitting, the red and parked rows, the last push result, the owner's words since the last sitting, and the count of open questions.
4. **One body line:** "Continue from the Next act above; read the brief by section: `<sections>`, and the act named."

The Next act is the first non-empty paragraph under `## Next act` in the newest `history/sittings/*-<id>.md` at main: the highest date, then the highest n as a number. The sitting opens its own charter with `claude-session charter open`, which numbers it and takes the `-<id>` suffix from `RELAY_ROW`; the relay never computes the path.

**The guards.** RULES are text, so three row guards hold them. `root-write` denies a git command whose working directory resolves inside the root checkout and outside the sitting's worktree: the hook input's cwd, or a `cd` or `pushd` target earlier in the same command. A target the guard cannot read denies. That covers one the command may change: after a word naming `HOME` or `CDPATH`, after an `ln` or `mv`, or one not there when the hook runs. `nested-run` also denies `relay words`, and the binary refuses it under `RELAY_ROW` or `CLAUDE_SESSION_HELPER`. An `Agent` call with `run_in_background` set is denied, as a background Bash command is today. `--allowedTools` gains `Agent`. Act 2's builder measures once whether `Agent` passes auto mode under `claude -p`, and whether the Workflow tool runs there in the foreground. Helpers are subagents only unless that measure admits the Workflow tool; if `Agent` is refused, helpers are off and RULES say so. A helper run as a `claude -p` process stays capped by `MaxHelpers` (2).

**The measure** (ar32a act 2, 2026-09-29). One `claude -p` helper in the helper guard's shape, asked for one foreground `Agent` call, got one: a general-purpose subagent answered, with no permission denial, in 2 turns for $0.74. The helper line admits no `--permission-mode`, so it ran in the account's default mode, not the spawn's `auto`. The Workflow tool is present, but it runs only in the background: it returns a task id and notifies. The documentation agrees ("Orchestrate subagents at scale with dynamic workflows", code.claude.com, as of 2026-10-14): a workflow runs in the background, and under `claude -p` Claude Code never shows the approval prompt but runs the Workflow call through the session's permission evaluation, so it starts only on a `Workflow` allow rule, auto mode's classifier, bypass mode, a PreToolUse hook that allows it, or a host's permission callback; a `-p` run stays open until its background subagents and workflows complete ("Run Claude Code programmatically", same date). The one foreground `Agent` call measured here is the case where Claude needs the subagent's result before it goes on. So helpers are on, and the Workflow tool stays off; ar32c need not add it.

## 4. When the relay restarts it

The cycle is: an orchestrator sitting, its gate and merge, a re-read of the queue at main, then every ready build row in queue order, one at a time, and the orchestrator again. When nothing runs and no build row is ready, the orchestrator is picked before the loop's `queue-empty` and `blocked` stops; those stops apply only once the orchestrator row has ended.

- **The sitting's id** is `<id>.<n>`: its branch, its worktree and its ledger key. n is one past the highest n the ledger holds for the row, across nights, so an id is never reused after `relay clean` removes its branch. Each sitting gets a fresh `RowPlan` with a new session id and a fresh `relay.Sitting`. The plan's existing-branch refusal and own-id landed test read `<id>.<n>`, never `<id>`. example-app's attended `orient` branch therefore matches nothing, and the owner merges or drops it before the first orchestrated night.
- **The first sitting** starts from the newest charter at main; with none, CONTEXT reads `none` and the brief's Act 0 applies.
- **`WAITING: <what>`** is a held state, not a stop. It holds the restart until a build row ends or the owner's words arrive. After `idle` (default 2 h) with neither, the night stops `waiting`.
- **`DONE: <why>`** ends the row once no build row it added is ready, running, or green and unmerged. Until then DONE reads as WAITING, and the next sitting merges those rows and writes DONE again. The night then stops `done`.
- **No progress.** The key is the Next act with a leading `RESPAWN: ` stripped and whitespace folded. A sitting is stuck when its merge changed no path outside `history/sittings/` and the key at main after it equals the key it started from. One stuck sitting is allowed, since an act may span a checkpoint; a second in a row stops the night `no-progress`. The picked-twice rule skips this row, and build rows keep it. A loop between two Next acts is not caught; `--max-usd` and `--until` bound it.
- **A red sitting** is parked with its reason, and the row stays parked for the rest of the day (example-ops "The gate, and what a red row does"). A later night that day in the same repo refuses to start it (the ledger is the account's, so the refusal reads only lines naming this root, or naming none), and `relay resume <id>.<n>` is the door into the sitting. As that entry says, a gate red moves the night on: the ready build rows run, and the night then stops `orchestrator-red`.
- **A merge that does not land.** A green sitting whose merge fails with a plain error stops the night `merge-held` and names the branch. Such errors include a dirty root, a red main, a moved main and `tool-moved`. The relay never restarts from a main that lacks the sitting's charter. A merge refused as today (`git-state-moved`, `merge-tree-differs`) reads red, as for any row. Its `sitting` line was written green before the merge, so the day's refusal also reads the refused `merge` line and the `stop` line's resume ids.

## 5. Its gate: what green means for a design sitting

Exit, commit, clean, origin and orphans run as today. Three checks are new, and touch changes:

- **charter:** the newest `history/sittings/*-<id>.md` at `Gate.Head` was added on this branch, is under 4,096 bytes and has a non-empty `## Next act`; else red `no-charter`.
- **queue:** `relay/queue.yaml` at `Gate.Head` loads and validates, and each added row's packet parses. Every row at main when the sitting began is unchanged, so a sitting only appends rows and never unparks one. The defaults, `words` and the orchestrator row equal the queue as the night began, never main's. So does each added row's `harness`, `device`, `model`, `merge` and agent-relay entry 1's copy-in field: each is unset or equal to the defaults. An added row's budget stays within the defaults'. Else red `queue-invalid`.
- **merged rows:** each merge on the branch's first-parent line has, as its other parent, a `Gate.Head` that a green build-row gate bound in this process. Any other parent, such as a helper's branch or a red row's commit, reds `row-unproven`. Each such merge must equal its automatic result (`git merge-tree` of its parents), so an edit made inside a merge commit also reds `row-unproven`. A row found green by an earlier process is re-gated at plan. Built by ar32b act 1: a green build-row gate notes its head (`night.NoteGreen(root, id, head)`, read back by `night.GreenHead`), and an orchestrator's gate notes none. A proven merge has exactly two parents, and `git merge-tree --write-tree` of the two exits 0 with the merge's tree. The merges, their `merge-tree` and the touch walk run in the gate's fresh repository. The re-gate at plan is not built yet (§11), so until it is, a merge of a row found green by an earlier process reds `row-unproven`.
- **touch:** the branch's own first-parent diffs, the proven merges exempt, lie inside the row's bounds. Each first-parent commit is read against its first parent (ar32b act 1), so a path changed and then changed back still reads outside. The bounds are every unguarded path plus the guarded paths `may_touch` names. The brief is guarded unless `may_touch` names it, so a sitting cannot rewrite its own instructions.

In an orchestrated night `relay/queue.yaml` is a pinned path for every build row: a build row that changes it reads red. Green merges on green, the row's `verify` line on the merged tree, as relay-r1 §6 step 7 does.

## 6. Build rows: added, run and merged

The orchestrator commits a packet under `design/packets/` and its row in `relay/queue.yaml` on its branch. It checks the row with `relay plan --queue relay/queue.yaml --rows <id>`, which inside a row takes no lock and measures nothing. There it reads packets from the worktree that holds the `--queue` file, since the new packet is only on the branch; outside a row they come from the root. After the orchestrator's merge, and only then, the relay loads the queue from the root, clean and on main. It writes a `queue-reread` line with the new hash, reads the Every session block again, and plans the added rows with `BuildPlan` under the night's name, measuring only them. The row and merge closures bind the new queue. A re-read that fails stops the night `queue-invalid`. Nothing is running at a re-read, since `--orchestrate` refuses `--parallel` above 1.

**The orchestrator's next sitting merges a branch-only build row, inside its own branch.** The row is listed in the next CONTEXT line. The sitting re-runs its acceptance lines in its worktree, including those that need a sibling repo beside the checkout, which the relay's gate clone cannot see. It merges with `git merge --no-ff <row>`, and the relay merges the orchestrator branch. So the relay stays main's only writer, and one verify on the merged tree covers several rows. A build row set to `merge-on-green` still lands by the relay. `night.landed` and `relay clean --merged` gain one hop: a row's tip is the later parent of a merge on a landed orchestrator branch's first-parent line. Merged worktrees and branches stay until `relay clean --merged`, as relay-r1 §8 says.

**Unmerged work at a stop.** At `--until`, `--max-usd`, `--max-sittings` or `relay stop`, a build row green on its branch stays there unmerged, and the stop's notice names it. The next night re-gates it at plan, as today. If main has moved, it reads red `main-moved` and is cut again as a new row.

**What ar32b built** (2026-09-29). The RULES' merge sentence replaces ar32a's "never merge" one, and CONTEXT names `green branches to merge: <ids>` after the red rows. Those are this night's build rows green with no `MergeSHA`, in queue order, and the template hash moves. `night.landed` takes the hop through a branch `<o>.<n>`, n a sitting's number, that itself landed on main; a landed branch that is no sitting's is no hop. After a sitting's merge and before its Next act is read, each green build row with no `MergeSHA` that now reads landed takes that merge's sha, so a `DONE:` ends. Each such row writes its own `merge` line, result `merged`, the sitting's merge sha and `via` the sitting, so `relay report` counts it merged and never lists it as waiting on a hand merge (post-review F1). `relay clean --merged` needed no change, since ancestry covers the hop, and a test pins that. The `stop` line and its notice name `unmerged`. An orchestrator row that names no `merge` merges on green over `branch-only` defaults, and `--orchestrate` accepts those defaults.

## 7. Questions, the owner's words, the full check and the push

**Questions.** The sitting uses `decisions new` and `decisions ask` and never calls the selector. After each merge the relay counts the open questions in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` at main: the `## <n>.` headings above the first `## Decided without asking` or `## Answered`. When the count rises, it sends a `question` notice at priority 4 with the body `open=<n> file=OPEN-QUESTIONS.md at=<sha12>`. As `noticeFields` guarantees, a notice never carries the question's text. An orchestrator night refuses to start without an ntfy topic (example-ops "How a row and a night stop").

**The owner's words** (fork 3). `relay words "<text>"` appends a `words` line to the control file, and `ReadControl` learns to read it. The next sitting's CONTEXT carries the text as the owner's words, and the sitting records each answer with `decisions answer`. The words also release a WAITING hold.

**The push: the relay pushes, and a child never does** (fork 2). With `push: main`, each orchestrator merge that landed a build row queues its sha for `push_check`. One check runs at a time: a newer sha replaces a queued one, and the running check finishes. The check runs in a detached linked worktree of the root. example-app's `hooks/gate-record` then writes its verdict into the common git dir, where the pre-push hook reads it. That worktree gets no relay pre-push deny and keeps the repo's configured hooks. When the check is green, the relay records the pushed ref as its own before it pushes. It then runs `git push origin <sha>:refs/heads/main` from that worktree, through the pre-push hook and never with `--no-verify`. The origin fact in `gather` and `MergeIfGreen`'s `remotesSame` read that record, beside `ownMerge` and `expectedMain`.

**The push's env.** A new effect, `OSGit.Push`, is the one git call outside `gitCmd`'s runner arguments, since it keeps `core.hooksPath`. The check and the push run under the pinned env, with the account's `HOME` in place of the relay home and the account's `SSH_AUTH_SOCK` added. That is a named departure from relay-r1 §6, "The gate reads a clone under a pinned env", for these two effects only. example-app needs both: its check reads `tla2tools.jar` from `$HOME/.local/lib`, and its origin is an ssh remote, which needs the account's ssh config and agent.

**Push results.** Each result is a `push-check` or `push` ledger line and a notice at priority 4. CONTEXT names the last one (sha, result and log path), so the next sitting can fix a red check. A red check pushes nothing, and the next landing tries a newer sha. A hook refusal reads `push-refused` and an auth or network failure `push-failed`; neither stops the night. A non-fast-forward can come only from another pusher, since the relay pushes in order; it reads `push-behind` and ends pushing for the night, and the night goes on. At `relay stop` or the night's end, the night waits for a check in flight up to one hour, then kills its process group and says so. `relay kill` kills it at once.

**The amendments** (fork 2). example-app "The relay cycle is example-app's own" changes in four clauses. The owner starts the night with `relay run --orchestrate`; the orchestrator no longer starts it in tmux. The orchestrator checks a row with the lock-free `relay plan --queue … --rows`, not a measuring plan. Green build rows merge during the night into the orchestrator's branch, which the relay merges, not after the night into main. The relay runs the full `make check` and pushes `main` through the pre-push gate. Its other clauses stand: HANDOFF to packets and rows, the queue's defaults, the acceptance lines checked with example-ops beside the checkout, and no child pushes. example-ops "The relay: one child, one worktree, one branch" gains one clause: the driver may push `main` after the full check when the row asks.

## 8. Starting, stopping, budgets and the live view

**The one command**, from the repo root: `relay run --orchestrate --max-usd 600`, in a terminal left open or a detached tmux session. It selects the orchestrator row and every unparked build row. It refuses without `--max-usd` or `--until`, since a standing row needs a bound. It also refuses without an ntfy topic, with `--parallel` above 1, and while the orchestrator row is parked that day. The account lock still allows one relay per account (relay-r1 §8).

**It stops** at `relay stop` (the running sitting finishes, gate and merge included) or `relay kill`; at `--until`, `--max-usd` or `--max-sittings`; at the new `done`, `waiting`, `no-progress`, `orchestrator-red`, `merge-held` or `queue-invalid`; and at every sitting failure that stops a night today. `done` with no row red exits 0, as `queue-empty` does; the other new stops exit 1.

**Budgets.** The model and budget come from the row: Fable 5.1, or Opus 5.5 on `[1m]`, with 400 turns and a 400k context ceiling (example-ops "The session budget"). The sitting is asked to stop at the relay's 90% soft line (example-ops "How a row and a night stop") and killed at `HardCap`, well under the 1M window. So it is never compacted: the relay's restart is the only way it continues. The soft note also fires at 90% of the row's usd, read from the turn lines' cost, since helpers spend from the same bound. Helpers' tokens count in the sitting's usage and cost, never its context. Up to eight subagents run at once (example-ops "The session budget").

**The live view.** `relay status` and its tmux windows show one orchestrator line: sitting number, state, context against the ceiling, turn, and the first 60 characters of the Next act. Build rows follow as today, then the last push (sha, result, minutes), the open questions, and the next stop. The ledger gains `queue-reread`, `push-check`, `push`, `question` and `words` lines.

## 9. Forks the design cannot settle

1. **Two repos at once.** The account lock allows one relay per account. example-ops "The relay: one child, one worktree, one branch" and "The session budget" allow one relay child at a time. Recommended: one night serves one repo, and the other repo's orchestrator stays attended; this costs nothing while example-ops stays attended under fork 4. The alternative is one night over several roots, with the lane passing at each sitting's end, one child at a time account-wide. One root then waits up to a sitting's 6 h wall. `night.Run` holds one root throughout, so that is a rewrite with its own record. A second `relay run` that joins a live night would undo relay-r1 §8's lock, which refuses any live holder with no bypass. A lock per root would reopen the fork-storm door AR20 and AR22 closed.
2. **Who pushes.** Recommended: the relay, as section 7 says, with its amendments and its departure from the pinned env. The alternative: nothing pushes at night, and the owner or the next attended sitting runs the full check and pushes.
3. **Answers during a night.** An edit to `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` in the root would block the next merge. Recommended: `relay words "<text>"` (section 7), refused inside a row or a helper, so only the owner's terminal speaks as the owner. The fallback always works: stop, answer, commit, start again.
4. **Writes to other repos.** A row's gate sees only its own branch, and the other-tree guard denies `git -C`. example-ops's program is cross-repo: it scaffolds repos, moves files with their history and registers each repo in the portfolio's registry. Recommended: an orchestrator row writes only its own repo, so example-ops stays attended for its moves. A sitting reads a sibling repo as plain files in that repo's working tree. It files a cross-repo ask in its own `history/sessions/` and never waits on one. The alternative is a row that declares the sibling repos it may write, each written in a relay worktree there and gated there; it needs its own record.

The build proceeds on these recommendations.
